# Tokens and value accrual

For any question shaped like "is this token worth anything", "should the token pump if the protocol grows", or "how do launches work". A token is not equity by default: it is whatever rights its contracts and governance actually give it. Start every token analysis by listing those rights, then follow the cash.

## The value accrual menu

Mechanisms by which protocol activity can reach a token, strongest to weakest:

1. Buybacks funded by revenue (protocol earns fees, buys the token on market: cash conversion, verifiable onchain; check it is revenue-funded and rule-based, not treasury-funded and discretionary).
2. Fee switch / revenue share to stakers (real yield paid in an external asset; check what fraction of fees, and whether it is active or merely possible: "the DAO could turn it on someday" is a call option, not cash flow).
3. Burn mechanics (supply reduction from activity; value accrues only if demand is independent of the burn story).
4. Staking for function (the token secures something and earns for it: insurance backstops, oracle bonding, sequencer rights: real work, real risk, priced accordingly).
5. Vote-escrow / gauge power (the token directs emissions or parameters; worth something when third parties pay for that direction: bribe markets make it measurable).
6. Fee discounts and access (utility that saves users money: creates demand proportional to usage, weakly).
7. Governance alone (an unpriced claim on future decisions: the weakest, and the default for most tokens).

The discriminating questions: is any mechanism LIVE today (dated), what is the annualized cash value reaching holders, what is that as a yield on market cap, and who can change the rules (the same governance the token supposedly controls)?

## Supply side: the launch mechanics that decide early price

Float vs FDV (fully diluted valuation): low float + high FDV means early price is set by a thin tradable sliver while insiders await unlocks; the unlock schedule IS the sell-pressure calendar: map it before any judgment. The observable extraction evidence, when it surfaces, is a private OTC (over-the-counter) menu: discounted locked-token deals, monthly-rate loans repayable in the token, "guaranteed discount" tranches, and unilateral vesting changes (a public sale's cliff moved after the fact). Any of these circulating is the launch-mechanics section talking out loud, and the information map that follows is: team knows the unlocks, market maker knows the positioning, OTC buyers know their cliffs, retail knows only price (calibration: the LAB investigation, May to July 2026; the trade side is references/trade-anatomy.md). Points and pre-launch farming convert to supply at TGE (token generation event). Market makers receive token loans with strike-priced options: their hedging is structural early sell flow. Listings, incentives, and airdrops are marketing spend paid in supply. The empirical base rates are brutal (most launches trade below their first prints within a year: verify current-cycle stats before citing), so the honest default for any new token is: distribution event first, investment second.

## Valuation without self-deception

Anchor on what is measurable: fee run-rate and its trend; the fraction reaching holders (accrual-adjusted yield on market cap); comparables on the same metrics; float-adjusted market cap vs FDV; treasury and runway. For any CROWDED name, add the funded-vs-borrowed test (framework: Delphi Digital's "The Crowded Book", Aug 2026; the authors disclosed possibly holding names they scored well): a bid is FUNDED when something durable pays for it (real cash flows into buybacks against a held float, organic demand, a supply sink that retires float) and BORROWED when it rests on leverage, momentum, and thin float. Score five things 0 to 5: revenue backing, float depth, holder breadth, leverage/reflexivity (can the structure run in reverse), and recovery quality after the last shock (the only outcome-based test). Corollaries: a buyback is only as good as what it buys against (revenue trend times float discipline: the same mechanic held one token and failed another whose insiders sold into it), and equity sitting above the token (a company raise that confirms shareholders come first) is a repricing event for the token's claim, not a detail. Narrative multiples on top of zero accrual are sentiment measurements, not valuations: say so. And the reflexive trap: token incentives that fund the yield that attracts the TVL that justifies the token is the circular structure behind most collapses: decompose it (concepts.md section 4, endogenous axis) before believing any growth chart.
