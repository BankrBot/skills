# Curation frameworks: how professional curators and allocators actually work

Load this for curator-process questions ("how do professional curators or allocators operate", "what should I ask a curator", "is this manager institutional-grade"), when playbook step 11 rates a manager, and when a user designs their own allocation process. Concepts section 3 defines curation; this file maps the professional landscape so an agent can score any manager BY THEIR PROCESS, not their brand. It distills the published frameworks of eleven firms (fetched August 2026): Clearstar, Steakhouse, Gauntlet, MEV Capital (the curator, unrelated to MEV the ordering phenomenon), KPK, Yearn, Armitage (Wintermute), Birch Hill, Noon, K3 Capital, and Spark's Liquidity Layer. Every framework below is self-published: an admission of method AND a marketing document, so part 4's verification asymmetry applies throughout.

## 1. Two functions, and the automated form (identify the role PER PRODUCT, not per firm)

CURATION parameterizes markets inside a venue: choosing collateral, caps, LLTVs (liquidation loan-to-value ratios), oracles, and allocations across markets. ALLOCATION sizes capital across curators and venues on behalf of a balance sheet or mandate: choosing WHOSE markets to trust and how much. These are functions, not firm types, and firms routinely hold both: Yearn documents the path explicitly (it first allocated its strategies into other curators' vaults, built monitoring to score those curators, then "took the next step" and became a curator itself); Spark allocates programmatically AND curates its own Morpho vaults; K3 curates public vaults while running $570M+ of proprietary delta-neutral portfolios (their May 2025 figure); KPK curates onchain while managing DAO treasuries; Steakhouse and Gauntlet curate while selling advisory and risk services. The vaults.fyi census's two curation types map onto the same axis: risk-parameter curation is the curator function, discretionary management is the allocator function.

The automated form: Spark's Liquidity Layer is a PROGRAMMATIC allocator. Governance pre-approves every venue via rate-limit keys (an unconfigured venue reverts), each limit regenerates linearly under a hard ceiling, every integration carries a slippage floor, OTC flows are bounded to one outstanding swap per exchange, and the threat model assumes the operational key is fully compromised, with a freezer role to cut it. Whether by humans or contracts, the allocator function is the same: venue selection plus exposure sizing under constraints.

Depositor consequences of stacking: a claim can now pass depositor → allocator → curator → venue → underlying, so ask three questions wherever both functions appear: SELF-DEALING (does the allocator route into its own curated vaults, on what terms), FEE STACKING (allocator fee on curator fee on venue spread: the take-rate map, concepts 8), and MANDATE COLLISION (does the firm's proprietary book: a market-making desk, a delta-neutral fund: see or trade against the flow its fiduciary-ish products create).

## 2. The common anatomy (where the eleven converge: safe to call industry practice)

Ten stages recur, whatever the branding: sourcing with minimum requirements; fit-screening against the mandate BEFORE deep work; graded due diligence in parallel tracks (asset, protocol, chain, plus cross-cutting security and governance); a rating gate that converts grades into a decision; an engagement loop for fixable failures; whitelisting of exactly the contracts to be called; monitoring designed FROM the assessment's findings; portfolio construction with tier-linked caps; execution controls; and re-rating on a clock and on events. The convergent design choices worth treating as the professional baseline: tiered risk classes tied mechanically to allocation caps (Clearstar's five tiers with 50% and 25% caps; KPK's A-to-D tiers; Yearn's 1-to-5 market tiers); exit liquidity BY CONSTRUCTION (Yearn caps any single market at 95% of vault assets; Noon sizes each position to the free-liquidity buffer the venue's rate model maintains: maximum exposure = (100% minus the interest-rate kink) times pool liquidity; KPK alerts at 97% utilization); liquidation-slippage simulation against real aggregated DEX depth (Yearn recomputes supply caps several times daily from 10+ venues and zeroes caps on extreme slippage; Birch Hill and Gauntlet simulate the same); worst-case-dominates scoring (KPK's overall rating equals its worst dimension; Clearstar's F-gate has no override and weights weakness extra; Birch Hill sets hard caps from collateral value-at-risk); and a two-speed automation gradient: automated response is reserved for the exploit case where speed beats judgment (Hypernative-style detection wired to deallocate at Clearstar, KPK, Armitage, Yearn), while everything ambiguous routes to humans, with soft (reversible) versus hard (cap-zeroing, timelocked to re-enable) shutdown paths.

## 3. The differentiators (each one is a question to ask ANY manager)

Published thresholds versus adjectives: Yearn publishes its optimizer rules, monitoring code, dashboards, and a public risk API; KPK publishes its scoring epistemology; Gauntlet and MEV Capital publish philosophy with fewer numbers; K3 publishes essentially nothing (a single 2025 forum post): under this file's method, ABSENCE OF A PUBLISHED PROCESS IS ITSELF A FINDING, leaving only track record and onchain artifacts to underwrite. Ask: where are your thresholds written down?

Scoring epistemology: KPK alone states that risks are scored on SEVERITY not probability ("probability is rarely knowable in DeFi and severity usually is"), that thin evidence counts AGAINST an asset, and that mitigants are recorded but never netted off scores. Ask: does weak evidence help or hurt a candidate in your process?

Observability as a gate: KPK scores whether a protocol's critical state transitions can even be watched onchain, and can decline assets that cannot be monitored. Ask: what would you not be able to see, and did that affect the decision?

Microstructure inputs: Birch Hill alone uses supplier and borrower concentration indices, liquidation-price clustering maps (death-spiral detection), oracle latency versus centralized-exchange flow, LP stickiness through past stress, market-maker service agreements as an allocation factor, and centralized-versus-decentralized exchange spread widening as a market-maker-failure alarm. Ask: what do you watch besides the venue's own dashboard?

Hard preconditions: Noon alone gates deployment on INSURANCE covering the relevant risks and prices its hurdle as T-bill plus insurance cost; it also publishes reviews of the curators it allocates to (an allocator underwriting its managers in public: reward it). Ask: what would stop you from deploying even at a great rate?

Operational honesty artifacts: Yearn's internal-versus-onchain cap staging (tighten internal caps instantly, push onchain later) is both clever and a disclosure that effective limits are not the visible ones. Clearstar's rules that raw agent output is never sent externally and that a named human signs every report are the agent-era accountability pattern. Ask: what does your tooling do without a human, and who signs?

## 4. The verification asymmetry (bias discipline for this whole file)

Split every framework's claims by checkability. Onchain-checkable: timelocks, multisig configurations, role holders, whitelist behavior, caps, whether exits actually fired in past incidents (time-to-exit remains the single best externally observable proof of process: concepts 3). Represented-but-unverifiable: review quality, dual sign-off actually happening, simulation discipline, "150 checks." Score the checkable claims by checking them; hold the rest as represented; and weight evidenced stress behavior above everything published. A calibration print for the genre: KPK's public tier table listed a certain synthetic-dollar receipt token as Tier C (issuer-run oracle, emerging) before that product's redemption halt in August 2026: published frameworks can be predictive; the point of this file is teaching an agent to read them, then verify the parts that can be verified.
